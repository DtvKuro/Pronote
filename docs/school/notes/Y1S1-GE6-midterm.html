1. Introduction to Art and Humanities

Art

  • From the Aryan "Ar" = "to put together", and the Latin "Ars" = "skill".
  • Covers visual, literary, musical, and performance arts.
  • Expresses aesthetic value through skill and imagination.

Humanities

  • From the Latin "Humanus" = "refined," "cultured," "human."
  • Studies the construct of man: its frailty and its improvement.
  • Investigates man's quest for answers to life's questions.
  • Understands expressions of man's feelings and thoughts; recognizes creative expression.
  • Emphasizes the dignity and worthiness of man.
  • Aims to shape human values and subjective energies (behavior, attitude, aspirations).

Importance of Humanities

  1. Man gains an understanding of himself.
  2. Man understands his nature.
  3. Man is given a measure of his own passion and desire.
  4. Man develops into a complete and social being.
  5. Man's behavior is regulated.

Basic Assumptions of Art (6)

  1. Art has been created by all people, at all times, in all countries, and it lives because it is liked and enjoyed.
  2. Art involves experience; there is no appreciation without experience.
  3. Art is not nature; nature is not art. Art is made by man.
  4. Art is everywhere.
  5. Art is man's oldest means of expression.
  6. Art is expression and communication.

Own example (#3): A sunset isn't art (nature). A painting of a sunset is art (made by a person).

2. Art Forms, Kinds, Types, and Functions

Art Forms (Ways of Expressing Oneself Through Art)

Form Meaning
Visual Arts Appeal to the sense of sight
Film Successions of frames/images that create an illusion of movement; today, recording movement with cameras plus script, music, and acting
Performing Arts Live art; the medium is mainly the human body, supported by props, visuals, or sound
Literary Arts Uses words to express and communicate emotions, in a unique style
Architecture The art of making beautiful buildings. Must have plan, construction, and design to be called architecture
Dance Series of movements following the rhythm of music
Poetry Performance Literary + performing art: poems performed before an audience
Theater Live performers presenting real or imaginary events on a stage
Applied Arts Brings beauty and comfort to useful everyday things: industrial, interior, fashion design

Two Kinds of Art

Fine / Aesthetic Arts (Major Arts) Utilitarian / Practical Arts (Minor Arts)
Focused on aesthetic value and enjoyment through the five senses Meant for practical use, but must still have artistic qualities
The Starry Night (Van Gogh), Swan Lake ballet, a classical concert Shell-craft, a sports car, a designed building

Two Types of Art

Functional Art Indirectly Functional Art
Art we use in daily life Art perceived through the senses
Tools, roads, bridges, buildings, furniture, kitchen utensils, coins, bills, dress, weapons Painting, music, sculpture, dance, literature, theater

Aristotle: every substance has an end, or telos (Greek for "purpose"). Telos is linked with function: for a thing to reach its purpose, it must fulfill its function.

Functions of Art (8)

Function Meaning Own example
Personal Serves the artist's own interests, motivations, and expression A diary-like self-portrait
Physical Satisfies man's physical needs; usable in everyday life A chair, a jacket
Social Addresses collective interest or raises awareness of social issues A mural against corruption
Aesthetic Makes people aware of beauty and enjoy it A landscape painting
Cultural Preserves, shares, and transmits culture across generations Tinalak weaving, folk dances
Political Aids a campaign or political agenda Campaign posters, protest art
Religious Used for/in religious activities Santo statues, church stained glass
Economic Art is sold/consumed; brings income to artists Selling commissioned portraits

Mnemonic: People Play Songs And Celebrate Peacefully, Really Enjoying = Personal, Physical, Social, Aesthetic, Cultural, Political, Religious, Economic

3. Philosophical Perspectives on Art

Guide questions: Is art more about emotion or meaning? Can something be art if no one understands it? Should art always be beautiful?

Philosopher View Key ideas
Plato Art as Imitation In The Republic, artists are imitators and art is mere imitation. Advised excluding art from the curriculum and banning artists from the ideal republic. Things in this world are copies of the originals in the world of forms. Suspicious of art because it (1) appeals to emotions, not reason, and (2) imitates rather than leads to reality. "Ideas are the ultimate reality."
Socrates Same as Plato Art is "an imitation of imitation"
Aristotle Art as Representation Plato's student; agreed art is imitation, but not with disgust. Art represents possible versions of reality: not reality as it is, but what might be
Immanuel Kant Art as Disinterested Judgment Judgment of beauty is the cornerstone of art; it can be universal despite being subjective. Judge art by its inherent beauty, not personal interests
Leo Tolstoy Art as Communication of Emotion Art communicates emotions the artist experienced, just as language communicates information. Fights for the social dimension of art: art is a mechanism of cohesion / social unity. It makes feelings of people from past to present accessible

Kant example (slides): "I like the painting" = personal interest (subjective). "This painting is beautiful" = disinterested judgment (claims universal beauty).

Plato vs Aristotle: Plato says imitation is bad (a poor copy). Aristotle says imitation is good, because it shows possibilities.

Memory trick: Plato = Poor copy · Aristotle = Alternate possibilities · Kant = Keep your bias out · Tolstoy = Transfer feelings

4. Subject and Content

Three basic components of a work of art (clues between the artwork and the viewer):

Component Meaning Question
Subject The visual focus or image in the artwork The "what"
Content The meaning communicated by the artist/artwork The "why"
Form How the elements and medium are put together The "how"

Types of Subject (a spectrum, not strict boxes)

Type Meaning Example
Representational (also figurative) Refers to real-world objects or events; figures are easy to recognize. Easier for the average viewer Leonardo da Vinci's Mona Lisa
Abstract Abstraction = filtering/distilling characteristics, leaving what is integral. Recognizable but simplified or distorted Picasso-style faces
Non-Representational No reference to the real world; only shapes, lines, colors to express feeling or concept Jackson Pollock's Number 1A

Some say all art has some abstraction, since every artist filters reality even in realistic works.

Sources of Subject

Nature · History · Greek and Roman mythology · Religion · Historically significant events · People
Contemporary artists can choose anything, from grand ideas to trivial things. Artists before didn't have that freedom.

Content: Three Levels of Meaning

Level Meaning Own example (Mona Lisa)
1. Factual Most basic: what is identifiable A woman smiling, seated before a landscape
2. Conventional Accepted interpretation using motifs, signs, themes, symbols Renaissance portrait; the smile as a symbol of mystery
3. Subjective Personal meaning a viewer derives from their own background "She reminds me of my lola"

5. Artists and Artisans

Artist Artisan (added definition)
Performs all forms of creative arts; creates art for art's sake with no ulterior motive; believed to create change in society through art A skilled craftsman who makes functional or decorative items by hand, often following traditional methods

Famous Artists (slides)

Artist Key facts
Vincent van Gogh (1853–1890) Dutch post-impressionist painter; one of the most influential in Western art. The Starry Night
Michelangelo di Lodovico Buonarroti Simoni (1475–1564) Italian sculptor, painter, architect, poet of the High Renaissance, born in the Republic of Florence. (Sistine Chapel ceiling, Creation of Adam)

Guilds

  • Guilds = a social fellowship/association with rules, customs, rights, and responsibilities.
  • Through lifetime commitment to a trade, an artisan builds great skill. A master artisan could hire apprentices to train under him.
  • In guilds, artistry and technology flourished under one root.

Importance

Artists Artisans
Help us envision thoughts that may not be tolerated in the social/political paradigm. "Art is the queen of all sciences communicating knowledge to all the generations of the world." — Leonardo da Vinci Make and install useful things for homes, offices, industries; fix things for people and organizations. Provide blue-collar jobs that reduce unemployment, poverty, and social vices, letting people learn vocational/technical skills

6. Managers, Curators, Art Dealers, and National Awards

Role Job
Gallery Manager/Owner Chooses and presents art for sale; manages both the creative and business sides; organizes exhibitions, private sales, art loans
Curator In charge of a collection in a museum or gallery: assembling, cataloguing, managing, presenting, displaying artworks and artifacts
Art Dealer Person or company that buys and sells art for profit

Memory trick: Manager = Markets the gallery · Curator = Cares for the collection · Dealer = Deals for profit

Orden ng Pambansang Alagad ng Sining (Order of National Artists)

  • The highest national recognition for Filipinos who made significant contributions to the arts: architecture and allied arts, broadcast arts, dance, film, literature, music, visual arts.
  • Administered jointly by the NCCA (National Commission for Culture and the Arts) and the CCP (Cultural Center of the Philippines); presented by the President.
  • First recipient: Fernando Amorsolo, painter, named in 1972 for Visual Arts; called the "Grand Old Man of Philippine Art."

Privileges of a National Artist

  1. Rank and title of National Artist, proclaimed by the President
  2. Insignia and a citation
  3. Lifetime emolument and benefits like the highest officers of the land:
    • a. ₱100,000 cash award (net of taxes) for living awardees
    • b. ₱75,000 for posthumous awardees (paid to legal heirs)
    • c. Monthly life pension, medical and hospitalization benefits
    • d. Life insurance for insurable awardees
    • e. State funeral and burial at the Libingan ng mga Bayani
    • f. A place of honor at national state functions and cultural events

Gawad sa Manlilikha ng Bayan (GAMABA)

Also called the National Living Treasures Award.

  • Began as a project of the Philippine Rotary Club Makati-Ayala in April 1992; institutionalized by Republic Act No. 7355.
  • Honors folk and indigenous artists who stay true to their traditions.
  • Tied to a program that transfers their skills to new generations and promotes the craft.
  • Administered by the NCCA, through the GAMABA Committee and an Ad Hoc Panel of Experts.
Awardee Known for
Ginaw Bilog Hanunuo Mangyan poet; master of Ambahan poetry
Lang Dulay T'boli weaver of tinalak (colorful abaca cloth)
Eduardo Mutuc From Apalit, Pampanga; religious and secular art in silver, bronze, and wood

National Artist vs GAMABA: National Artist = achievement in fine/modern arts. GAMABA = traditional/folk artists keeping heritage alive.

Art Production Process

Stage What happens
1. Pre-production Planning: preparing all elements; outlining what to accomplish so time is used efficiently
2. Production Using the plan, the artist manipulates the medium to execute the artwork
3. Post-production After completion: the work is set, modified, displayed, transported, or promoted

7. Medium and Techniques

  • Medium: the material or substance a work is made of. Through it, artists express feelings and ideas.
  • Technique: the way the artist uses and manipulates materials to achieve the desired effect and meaning, according to personal style.

Classification by Medium

Class Meaning
Visual Arts Mediums can be seen and occupy space
Auditory Arts Mediums can be heard and are expressed in time
Combined Arts Mediums can be seen and heard; exist in both space and time

Visual arts, two groups

Two-dimensional Three-dimensional
Flat surface; vertical and horizontal character of the picture plane Occupies space: height, width, depth
Painting, drawing, printmaking, photography Sculpture, architecture, landscape, industrial design, crafts (furniture)

Own examples: Music on Spotify = auditory. A movie or a concert with a light show = combined.

Artists and Their Mediums

Artist Medium
Sculptor Metal, wood, stone, clay, glass (3D art)
Architect Wood, bamboo, bricks, stones, concrete, building materials (3D art)
Painter Pigments (watercolor, oil, acrylic, ink) on a flat ground (wood, canvas, paper, stone wall)
Musician Sound and instruments, including the human voice
Dancer The body and its movements to music; can tell stories or convey abstract ideas
Theatre Artist Integrates all the arts: stage, production design, performance, script
Photographer & Filmmaker The camera to record the world; filmmakers combine production design, sound, performance, screenplay

Medium Technique
