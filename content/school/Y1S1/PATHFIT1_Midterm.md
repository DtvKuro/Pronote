## 1. Nutrition

**Nutrition** means eating the right amount and type of nutrients to fuel your body. It is the cornerstone of any physical conditioning program.

- **Nutrients**: substances that give the body **structure**, **function**, or **energy**.
- **Essential nutrients**: nutrients that **must come from food** because the body can't make enough of them.

### Why proper nutrition matters

| Impact | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Physical health** | Healthy weight, muscle repair, stronger immune system. Lowers risk of heart disease, type 2 diabetes, some cancers. |
| **Energy & performance** | Carbs, protein, and fat fuel daily activity and workouts; less fatigue. |
| **Mental well-being** | Better mood and thinking; lower risk of mental health conditions. |
| **Recovery** | Protein repairs muscles after exercise, so less soreness and faster recovery. |

### The Five Food Groups

| Group | Job | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Carbohydrates** | **Main energy source**; base of most meals | Rice, bread, potatoes, cereal, fruit |
| **Protein** | **Builds and repairs** tissue and muscle | Chicken, fish, eggs, legumes, nuts |
| **Fats** | Energy, protects organs, insulation, helps absorb some vitamins. Choose **unsaturated** fats. | Avocado, nuts, vegetable oil (limit fried food, butter, fatty sausages) |
| **Vitamins & Minerals** | **Micronutrients** that regulate body processes | Vitamin C (citrus), iron (spinach), calcium (milk) |
| **Water** | Regulates temperature, carries nutrients, flushes waste | At least **8 glasses a day** |

**Proper food setup** means balancing the groups in every meal.
Example: grilled chicken (protein) + brown rice (carbs) + steamed broccoli and carrots (vitamins/minerals).

> **Own example:** A student's typical *silog* breakfast: rice = carbs, egg = protein, oil used for frying = fat. The missing groups are **vitamins/minerals** (add fruit or tomatoes) and **water**.

## 2. Physical Fitness Components

**Physical fitness** is the body's ability to do daily tasks **with vigor and alertness, without excessive fatigue**. It needs regular exercise, a healthy diet, and enough rest.

There are two categories: **Health-Related (HRF)** and **Skill-Related (SRF)**.

### A. Health-Related Fitness (HRF): 5 components
*These relate to overall health.*

| Component | Meaning | Test / Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Body Composition** | Ratio of **lean mass** (muscle, bone, organs) to **fat mass** | **BMI** |
| **Cardiovascular Endurance** | Heart, lungs, and blood vessels supplying oxygen to muscles over a **long period** | **3-Minute Step Test** (heart rate recovery) |
| **Muscular Strength** | **Maximum force** in a **single** effort | **Basic Plank** (core) |
| **Muscular Endurance** | **Repeated** contractions over a long period | **Push-ups** |
| **Flexibility** | **Range of motion** around a joint | **Sit-and-Reach** (hamstrings, lower back), **Zipper Test** (shoulders) |

> **Strength vs. Endurance:** Strength is lifting something heavy **once**. Endurance is doing something **many times**.

#### BMI formula

**BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)²**

> **Own example:** 60 kg, 1.65 m tall
> 1.65² = 2.7225 → 60 ÷ 2.7225 = **22.0** (normal)

**(added)** Standard BMI categories:

| BMI | Category |
|---|---|
| below 18.5 | Underweight |
| **18.5 – 24.9** | **Normal** |
| 25 – 29.9 | Overweight |
| 30 and above | Obese |

### B. Skill-Related Fitness (SRF): 6 components
*These matter for sports performance.*

| Component | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Speed** | Covering a distance in a **short time** | 50-meter sprint |
| **Agility** | **Changing body position** quickly with control | Shuttle run, cone drill |
| **Power** | Max force in the **shortest time** (**strength + speed**) | Volleyball spike jump, standing long jump |
| **Balance** | Staying in **equilibrium** while still or moving | Standing on one leg, balance beam |
| **Reaction Time** | Time to **respond to a stimulus** | Runner reacting to the starting pistol |
| **Coordination** | Using body parts together **smoothly** | Dribbling a basketball while running |

> **Mnemonic for SRF:** **S**illy **A**pes **P**lay **B**all **R**eally **C**almly = Speed, Agility, Power, Balance, Reaction time, Coordination

## 3. Understanding Your Training Load

| Term | Meaning | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| **PMHR** (Personal Maximal Heart Rate) | Highest heart rate you can reach | **220 − age** |
| **THR** (Target Heart Rate) | Heart rate range to aim for while exercising | **Moderate = 65% – 75% of PMHR** |
| **RPE** (Rate of Perceived Exertion) | **Subjective** scale of how hard you *feel* you're working; no heart-rate monitor needed | (added) Often rated 1–10: 1 = very easy, 10 = max effort |

**Handout example (age 20):** PMHR = 220 − 20 = **200 bpm**, THR = 200 × 0.65 to 200 × 0.75 = **130 – 150 bpm**

> **Own example (age 18):**
> PMHR = 220 − 18 = **202 bpm**
> Low end: 202 × 0.65 = **131.3**
> High end: 202 × 0.75 = **151.5**
> THR ≈ **131 – 152 bpm**

## 4. Exercise Programs

Exercise is prescribed by **intensity, duration, and frequency**.

- **Exercise**: a proper selection of activities **adapted to a person's age, condition, body type, and needs**, done at the right time and intensity.
- **Physical Exercise**: any bodily activity that **enhances or maintains** fitness, health, and wellness. It helps prevent **"diseases of affluence"** such as heart disease, cardiovascular disease, diabetes, and obesity.

### Types of Exercise

| Type | Meaning | Example (own) |
|---|---|---|
| **Aerobic** | **Moderate** intensity, **long** duration. "Aerobic" = **"with oxygen"** | Jogging 30 min, Zumba |
| **Anaerobic** | **High** intensity, **short** duration | 100 m sprint, heavy lifting |
| **Low Intensity** | Light activities | Light walk, stretching, beginner yoga, household chores |
| **Isometric** | Muscles **tense without moving** the joint (handout: "static", "no motion") | Plank, wall sit, pushing against a wall |
| **Isotonic** | Muscles **move through a range** against resistance (handout: "shifting of resistance") | Squats, bicep curls, push-ups |
| **Relaxation** (added definition) | Lowers tension and stress | Deep breathing, slow stretching |
| **Therapeutic** (added definition) | Restores movement or strength, often after injury | Rehab exercises for a sprained ankle |

> **Father of Aerobics:** **Dr. Kenneth H. Cooper**, a physician and former Air Force flight surgeon. He coined "aerobics" and published the book *Aerobics* in **1968**, promoting exercise for disease prevention and cardiovascular fitness.

### Phases of an Exercise Program

| Phase | Duration | Purpose | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Warm-up** | **5–10 min** | Raises body temperature, loosens muscles, **prevents injury** | Brisk walk + arm circles, leg swings |
| **2. Workout** | Main part | Tailored to your goals | 30-min run, dance class, circuit training |
| **3. Cool-down** | **5–10 min** | **Gradually lowers** heart rate and temperature; static stretches reduce soreness | Slow walk + holding stretches |

## 5. Types of Body Movement

### A. Appendicular Movements
Movements of the **arms and legs** (appendages) and the girdles connecting them to the axial skeleton. They usually come in **opposite pairs**.

| Movement | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Flexion** | **Decreases** the angle at a joint | Bending the elbow |
| **Extension** | **Increases** the angle at a joint | Straightening the elbow |
| **Abduction** | Moves a limb **away** from the body's midline | Raising your arm to the side |
| **Adduction** (added) | Moves a limb **toward** the midline | Lowering your arm back to your side |
| **Medial (Internal) Rotation** | Rotates a limb **toward** the midline | Toes pointing inward |
| **Lateral (External) Rotation** | Rotates a limb **away** from the midline | Toes pointing outward |

> **Memory trick:** **Add**uction = **add** the limb back to the body. **Ab**duction = **ab**sent from the body (taken away).

**Specialized movements**

| Movement | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Circumduction** | **Circular** movement combining flexion, extension, abduction, and adduction. Example: arm circles |
| **Pronation** | Forearm turns so the palm faces **down / backward** |
| **Supination** | Forearm turns so the palm faces **up / forward** (like holding a bowl of **soup**) |
| **Inversion** | Sole of the foot turns **inward** |
| **Eversion** | Sole of the foot turns **outward** |

> **Memory trick:** **Sup**ination = holding **soup**, palm up.

### B. Axial Movements
Movements done on a **stationary base** (you stay in one spot). They move around the body's **central axis / spine**.

| Movement | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Bending** | Flexing or extending a joint | Forward bend, squat |
| **Stretching** | Lengthening a muscle or body part | Side stretch |
| **Twisting** | Rotating the torso around the spine | Torso twist |
| **Swinging** | Moving a body part in an **arc** | Arm swings |
| **Shaking** | Rapid, **vibrating** movement | Shaking out your legs after a set |

> **Appendicular vs. Axial:** Appendicular is about **which part** moves (the limbs). Axial is about **moving in place** around the spine.

## 6. Step Aerobics Terminology

### Leg Movements

| Term | How it's done |
|---|---|
| **V-Step** | Step **up wide**, step **down narrow**. Your feet trace a **"V"** |
| **A-Step** | Reverse of V-step: start **narrow**, step up **wide**, step down **narrow** |
| **Lunge** | Step one foot **back** and bend both knees (platform lunge: one foot on the step) |
| **Knee Lift** | Lift one knee **toward the chest** while standing on the other leg |
| **Leg Curl** | Bend the knee to bring the **heel toward the glutes** |

### Arm Movements

| Term | How it's done | Targets |
|---|---|---|
| **Bicep Curl** | Bend elbows, hands toward shoulders | Biceps |
| **Tricep Extension** | Straighten arms **behind** you from a bent position | Triceps |
| **Overhead Press** | Push hands straight **up** overhead | Shoulders |
| **Lateral Raise** | Lift arms **out to the sides** to shoulder level | Shoulders |
| **Front Raise** | Lift arms **straight in front** to shoulder level | Shoulders |
| **Chest Press** | Push hands **out in front** of the chest | Chest |
| **Punch** | Quick, straight arm movement: forward, side, or overhead | Arms, shoulders |
| **Row** | Pull elbows **back** like pulling a rope | Back |
