## 1. What Is Communication?

**Etymology:** from the Latin **"communis"** (*common*, "belonging to many") and **"communico"** (*to confer with others*).

**Communication** is the **process of exchanging information, ideas, thoughts, and feelings** between individuals or groups, **verbally or non-verbally**. It's the mutual exchange of understanding by any effective means.

- **Components:** a **sender** conveys a message; a **receiver** interprets it.
- **Importance:** builds relationships, helps understanding, achieves goals in personal, academic, and professional life.

### Principles and Characteristics of Communication

| Communication is... | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Schemata-driven** | We interpret messages using our **prior knowledge and experiences** (schemata) |
| **Symbolic** | Uses **symbols** (words, gestures) that stand for ideas |
| **Developmental / progressive** | Skills **grow and improve** over time |
| **An interpretative act** | Receivers **interpret** meaning; it's not just passed along |
| **Always results in something** | Every message has an **effect** |
| **A process** | Ongoing and dynamic, not a one-time event |
| **Not a guaranteed direct link between two minds** | What you mean isn't automatically what they understand |
| **Irreversible** | Once said, it **can't be taken back** |
| **Ethical** | Carries moral responsibility |
| **Active, powerful, forceful** | Can move, change, or hurt people |
| **Contextual** | Meaning depends on the **situation** |
| **Influenced by media and technology** | Channels shape how we communicate |

> **Own example (irreversible):** You can delete a tweet, but people already saw it. Screenshots exist.

## 2. Elements of Communication

| Element | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Sender** | The one who **initiates**, **encodes**, and sends the message |
| **Message** | The **information, idea, or feeling** being communicated (verbal or non-verbal) |
| **Medium / Channel** | The **method or platform** used: speaking, writing, digital |
| **Receiver** | The one who **receives and decodes** the message |
| **Feedback** | The receiver's **response**, showing whether the message was understood |
| **Noise** | Any **interference** that distorts the message: background noise, language barriers, misunderstandings |
| **Context** | The **situation and environment** that affects interpretation |

### The Communication Process

```
SOURCE → ENCODING → MESSAGE → CHANNEL → DECODING → RECEIVER
   ↑                                                    │
   └──────────────────── FEEDBACK ◄─────────────────────┘
                 (all inside a CONTEXT; NOISE can interfere anywhere)
```

| Step | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **1. Source** | The sender, starting with an **idea** |
| **2. Encoding** | Turning thoughts into **verbal or nonverbal symbols** |
| **3. Message** | The idea the sender wants to convey |
| **4. Channel** | The method used to transmit it |
| **5. Decoding** | The receiver's **mental processing** of the message |
| **6. Receiver** | The person/group who gets the message |
| **7. Feedback** | The receiver's **response or reaction** |
| **8. Context** | The **situation** where it all happens |

**Lesson diagram (step by step):** **Developing an idea** → **Encoding** → **Transmitting** → **Reception** → **Decoding** → **Acceptance or rejection** → **Using the information** → **Feedback** to the sender, with **noise** affecting every stage.

> **Own example:** You want to invite a friend to eat (idea) → you type "kain tayo?" (encoding) → send via Messenger (channel) → your friend reads it (decoding) → replies "G!" (feedback). A bad signal that delays the message = **noise**.

## 3. Models of Communication

| Model | How it works | Feedback? | Own example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Linear** | **One-way**: sender → message → receiver. Receiver decodes **without immediate feedback** | **No** | Radio/TV broadcast, a billboard |
| **Interactive** | **Two-way, taking turns**: receiver **gives feedback** to the sender | **Yes** (not simultaneous) | Texting back and forth, email |
| **Transactional** | **Both** are **sender and receiver at the same time**; continuous exchange within a **context** | **Yes, simultaneous** | Face-to-face conversation (you talk while seeing their reactions) |

> **(added)** These are commonly credited to: **Linear**: Shannon & Weaver (also Aristotle, Lasswell). **Interactive**: Schramm (Osgood-Schramm). **Transactional**: Barnlund.

> **Memory trick:** **Linear** = a **line** (one direction). **Interactive** = **ping-pong** (taking turns). **Transactional** = a **dance** (both moving at once).

## 5. Types of Communication

| Type | Meaning | Lesson example |
|---|---|---|
| **Verbal** | **Spoken or written words**, face-to-face, phone, or digital | A manager presents; team members take notes or ask questions (feedback) |
| **Non-verbal** | **Body language, facial expressions, gestures, posture, eye contact**. Complements or contrasts with words | A teacher **nodding and smiling** while praising a student reinforces the praise |
| **Written** | Messages through **writing**, formal or informal; often for **documentation** | A company sends a formal report to stakeholders |
| **Visual** | **Images, charts, graphs, infographics**; improves understanding and retention of complex info | A marketer uses infographics to show sales trends |

### Forms of Non-Verbal Communication

| Form | Meaning | Own example |
|---|---|---|
| **Paralanguage** | **Extra sounds** that go with spoken words (tone, pitch, "uhm," sighs) | Saying "okay..." in a flat tone means you're *not* okay |
| **Proxemics** | **Space/distance** that shows your thoughts or feelings | Standing close to a friend vs keeping distance from a stranger |
| **Time (Chronemics)** | Your willingness or hesitance **to wait** shows patience or impatience | Always arriving late sends a message |
| **Physical appearance & object language** | **Dress, body type, appearance, architecture, surroundings** | Wearing a suit to an interview shows seriousness |

## 6. Barriers to Effective Communication

| Barrier | Meaning |
|---|---|

| **Misunderstanding** | Misinterpretation from **unclear or ambiguous** language |
| **Emotional barriers** | **Anger, stress**, etc. affect sending and receiving |
| **Cultural differences** | Different **norms and practices** lead to miscommunication |

### Communication Contexts

| Context | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Situational** | The **psycho-social "where"** (the social situation: a date, a meeting, a funeral) |
| **Environmental** | The **physical "where"** (a classroom, a noisy mall) |

### Cultural Differences in Gestures

| Gesture | Positive meaning | Negative meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Thumbs up** | Approval/agreement in many **Western** cultures | An **insult** in **Bangladesh**; highly offensive in parts of the **Middle East** |
| **OK sign** | "Everything's fine" in **English-speaking** countries | **Rude** in **Brazil**. In the **1950s, Richard Nixon** used it in Brazil and caused offense |

## 7. Ethics in Communication

### Morals vs Ethics

| Morals | Ethics |
|---|---|
| **Personal** codes of conduct | **Societal** standards of good and bad |
| Individual rules, **not imposed** on others | Rules accepted by society, **imposed on everyone** |

> **Own example:** Not eating meat is a **moral** choice (personal). Not plagiarizing is an **ethical** standard (school rule for everyone).

### Ethical Principles of Communication (9)

| Principle | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **1. Mutuality** | Consider **others' needs** as well as your own |
| **2. Individual dignity** | Don't **embarrass** others, even if their ideas differ |
| **3. Accuracy** | Give **correct information**; don't spread fake news |
| **4. Access to information** | Let others access information; **don't use it as leverage** |
| **5. Accountability** | Take responsibility for the **consequences** of what you say |
| **6. Audience responsibility** | **Receivers** have ethical duties too (listen, verify, don't spread) |
| **7. Relative truth** | Respect others' **points of view** and different realities |
| **8. End vs means** | **The end doesn't justify** unethical or illegal means |
| **9. Use of power** | People in **authority** have **greater responsibility** |

> **Mnemonic:** **M**y **D**og **A**te **A**ll **A**unt **A**nna's **R**ice **E**very **P**m: Mutuality, Dignity, Accuracy, Access, Accountability, Audience, Relative truth, End vs means, Power

### Guidelines for Ethical Communicators
Ethical communicators:
- are **respectful** of their audiences
- consider the **consequences** of their communication
- **respect truth**
- use information **properly**
- **do not falsify** information
- respect others' **right to information**

> **Ethical communication enhances human worth and dignity** by fostering **truthfulness, fairness, responsibility, integrity, and respect** for self and others.

## 9. Language and Communication

### What Is Language?
According to linguists, it's only **language** if it has:
1. A **system of rules** (**grammar**)
2. A **sound system** (**phonology**)
3. A **vocabulary** (**lexicon**)

> **Humans are the only species that has "language."**

### How Do People Understand Language?

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Language acquisition** | **Firsthand**, consistent **exposure** to a language (natural, like a child) | Picking up Filipino at home |
| **Mother tongue** | The **first** language you acquired and use | Filipino or English, depending on your environment |
| **Second language** | Another language learned **after** your mother tongue | English for many Filipinos |
| **Language learning** | **Formal or informal study** of a language | Schooling, reading a dictionary |
| **Foreign language** | A language not used in your community | Japanese for most Filipinos |
| **Speech community** | People who **share** vocabulary, sounds, and grammar, so they understand each other | Filipino speakers |

### Language Acquisition vs Language Learning

| Acquisition | Learning |
|---|---|
| **Subconscious** | **Conscious** |
| **Informal** | **Formal** |
| Needs a **natural** environment | Needs a **formal** environment |
| Focus on **communication** | Focus on **form** (grammar) |
| **Faster** | **Slower** |
| **Passive** | **Active** |
| **Mother tongue** | Mother tongue **+ second language** |
| **Practice** (language in use) | **Theory** (language analysis) |
| **Implicit** | **Explicit** |
| **Automatic** | **Learned** |
| **Personal** | **Technical** |
| Priority on **spoken** language | Priority on **written** language |

> **Own example:** A child learns Tagalog just by hearing family talk = **acquisition**. You studying Japanese grammar from a textbook = **learning**.

### Fixing Language Confusion

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Language contact** | **Slowly learning** a language through **constant exposure** while communicating. You develop other ways to understand each other, sometimes a mixed form only you two understand |
| **Language change** | The **result** of language contact: you **fully learn** the language through communicating with its speakers |

> *"Remember that language, to be alive, needs to be used."*

### Language vs Communication
- **Language** is the **tool** of communication.
- **Communication** is the **process** of transferring messages.
- Communication **doesn't exist without language**.

### Why Is Communication Important?
1. It's the key to **good relationships**.
2. It helps you **gather information**.
3. It lets you **connect and form lasting bonds**.
4. It helps you **express emotions**.
5. We are **social beings**; communication helps us live.

### How to Overcome Shyness

| Step | Tips |
|---|---|
| **1. Listen** | A good communicator is a **good listener**. Show interest. React sometimes ("I see," "wow," "hmm") but don't overdo it. Listening = learning; talk once you're warmed up |
| **2. Ask questions and slowly build** | Ask **follow-up, elaboration, and clarifying** questions to gather material. More questions = longer conversation. Keep them coherent. No need to "reinvent the wheel" |
| **3. Relate** | Share a **similar experience** if you have one. It brings fresh perspectives. **Don't invent things** or seem desperate |
| **4. Express yourself** | Be you. Use **facial expressions and gestures**; smile so you don't look stiff. Body language adds another layer to your message |

> **Mnemonic:** **L**et **A**ll **R**elax & **E**njoy = Listen, Ask, Relate, Express

## 10. Robert Craig's Seven Traditions of Communication

**Robert Craig**: an **American communication theorist**. In his article **"Communication Theory as a Field,"** he argued that many communication theories can be used in dialogue, and proposed **7 traditions**.

| # | Tradition | Communication as... |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Socio-Psychological** | **Interaction and influence** |
| 2 | **Cybernetic** | A **system of information processing** |
| 3 | **Rhetorical** | **Artful public address** |
| 4 | **Semiotic** | The process of **sharing meaning through signs** |
| 5 | **Socio-Cultural** | The **creation and enactment of social reality** |
| 6 | **Critical** | A **reflexive challenge of unjust discourse** |
| 7 | **Phenomenological** | The **experience of self and others through dialogue** |

> **Mnemonic:** **S**ome **C**ats **R**un, **S**ome **S**leep, **C**ats **P**urr = Socio-psych, Cybernetic, Rhetorical, Semiotic, Socio-cultural, Critical, Phenomenological

### 1. Socio-Psychological Tradition: Interaction and Influence
- **Scientific** perspective; looks for **cause-and-effect** relationships to **predict** what happens when people communicate.
- Founded by **Carl Hovland** (experimental research on the effects of communication).
- Hovland's framework: **"Who says what to whom and with what effect."**
- Focuses on **patterns of interaction**. Reality is **constructed** through communicating in groups and cultures.
- Focuses on the **internal judgment** of a person toward a message; **attitude change** is the goal of persuasion.
- Researchers studied: (1) likelihood to change position, (2) direction of attitude change, (3) tolerance of other positions, (4) level of commitment.
- Concentrates on the **source**, the **content** of persuasive messages, and **audience characteristics**. The source's **expertise/credibility** matters.

**Branches**

| Branch | Focus |
|---|---|
| **Behavioral** | **Stimulus → response**: how people actually behave |
| **Cognitive** | **Mental operations** in managing information that lead to behavior (more popular today; behavioral is seen as too simple) |
| **Communibiology** | Communication from a **biological** perspective |

**Stimulus → Response:** *what triggers impressions* → *desired associations about something*

**Examples**
- **Cadbury:** A **credible** celebrity visited the factory and documented how the chocolates are made to calm fears about worms. **Who** (credible artist) says **what** (chocolates are safe) to **whom** (worried consumers) with **what effect** (anxiety quelled).
- **Doctors** appearing in ads for a product.
- Asking your partner "Do you love me?" now and again in 3 years; "Will my best friend still be my best friend in 10 years?"

### 2. Cybernetic Tradition: Information Processing
- The study of **information processing, feedback, control**, and communication **systems**.
- **"Cybernetics"** was coined by MIT scientist **Norbert Wiener**. The model was established by **Claude Shannon**.
- Communication is **goal-oriented**: was the message received with its intended meaning?
- **Feedback** is very important.
- **Noise** is the **"enemy of information"**; it disrupts the channel's capacity between sender and receiver.
- **Three components:** (1) the **goal** (message), (2) the **difference** between the goal and what was actually conveyed, (3) **feedback**.
- The message's **end meaning usually doesn't match** the intended one; the gap is reduced by **control**.
- **Redundancy** (repeating) reduces uncertainty; the more it's repeated, the clearer it becomes.

**Examples:** the **telephone game** ("Pass the message"), **gossip** (the story changes as it spreads), **paraphrasing** written/oral works.

### 3. Rhetorical Tradition: Artful Public Address
- **Rhetoric**: the art of using **all available means of persuasion**: arguments, organization of ideas, language, and **delivery in public speaking**.
- Classical sources: **Plato, Aristotle, Cicero, Quintilian** (Greco-Roman rhetors).
- Explains why **public discourse** matters and that communication skill can be **improved** through study.
- It's best to hear **both sides** before judging. Rhetoric is necessary even if often done poorly or harmfully.

**Socio-Psychological vs Rhetorical**

| Socio-Psychological | Rhetorical |
|---|---|
| Influence through **observation and scientific means** | Influence and persuasion through **public discourse (public speaking)** |

**Features of the Rhetorical Tradition**
1. **Speech separates humans from animals** (Cicero: what else could gather scattered people into a society?).
2. **Public speech in a democratic forum** solves political problems better than decrees or violence.
3. An **orator** influences a **wide audience** through persuasive discourse (public speaking is **one-way**).
4. **Rhetoric training** is the basis of a **leader's education** (speaking loudly without amplifiers).
5. Stresses the **power and beauty of language** to move emotions. **Rhetoric is an art, not a science.**
6. Public speaking was a **man's domain** until the 19th century (women had little chance to speak publicly).

**Examples:** the President's **SONA**, a student's **declamation**, a class president **announcing** tasks to the class.

### 4. Semiotic Tradition: Sharing Meaning Through Signs
- **Semiotics**: the study of **verbal and nonverbal signs** that stand for something else, and how their interpretation affects society.
- **Sign**: anything that stands for something else (fever = sign of infection; birds flying south = winter; an arrow = direction).
- **Symbols**: **arbitrary** words/signs with **no natural connection** to what they describe; meaning is **learned** in a culture.
- **Words** are symbols; they mean nothing on their own. **"Meanings reside in people, not in words."**
- Words are **multidimensional** and depend on context. *"Intimacy"* can mean self-disclosure, emotional expressiveness, physical contact, trust, support, sexual contact, or shared activities.

**Examples:** "Love," 🙂 **smile emoji**, 👍 **thumbs up**, ✌️ **peace sign**, **"Hala!"**, eyes, hands, **planet symbols** (☉ sun, ♀ Venus). Each can mean different things to different people.

> **Own example:** 🙂 is a friendly smile to your parents, but some Gen Z users read it as **passive-aggressive**.

### 5. Socio-Cultural Tradition: Creating Social Reality
- As people talk, they **produce and reproduce culture**.
- We think words reflect reality, but this tradition says it's **the other way around**: **language shapes our view of reality**.
- **Sapir–Whorf hypothesis (linguistic relativity):** the **structure of a culture's language shapes what people think and do**.
  > *"The 'real world' is to a large extent unconsciously built upon the language habits of the group."*
- Through language, **reality is produced, maintained, repaired, and transformed**.
- **Intertwined with the semiotic tradition.** Semiotic = symbols are arbitrary and up to interpretation. Socio-cultural = we **learn** meanings **through communication** in our culture, and these shape us as we grow.

**Examples:** a **red** stop sign means halt; **green** = go; wearing **glasses** = smart; the word "strong"; **rice** as a daily staple in Filipino culture.

> **Own example:** Filipinos have many words for rice (*palay, bigas, kanin*) because rice is central to our culture. English just says "rice."

### 6. Critical Tradition: Challenging Unjust Discourse
- Comes from the **Frankfurt School**, German researchers who critically examined **Karl Marx's** views. They rejected Marxism's economic determinism but kept its **social criticism**.
- Focus: history as the **unjust distribution of power and suffering**.
- **Control of language perpetuates power imbalances.** *"A language that is dominant in the conversation has the power to control."*

**Features**
1. **Control of language** keeps power imbalances; critical theorists condemn words that block emancipation.
2. **Blind reliance on the scientific method**: science isn't as value-free as it claims.
3. **Mass media** ("culture industries": TV, film, music, print) **dulls sensitivity to repression** by reproducing the dominant ideology.

**Examples:** one person **dominating** a conversation; a classmate **peer-pressured** into being class officer; a TV show where one group can joke about another but not the reverse; slurs that are acceptable only within a group.

> **Own example:** In a group chat, the loudest member decides everything and others' ideas get ignored. That's a **power imbalance** in discourse.

### 7. Phenomenological Tradition: Experience of Self and Others
- **Phenomenology**: analysis of **everyday experience** from the standpoint of **the person living it**.
- Emphasizes people's **perception and interpretation** of their **own subjective experience**.
- An individual's **story** matters more than any research hypothesis.
  > **Carl Rogers:** *"Neither the Bible nor the prophets—neither Freud nor research—neither the revelations of God nor man—can take precedence over my own direct experience."*
- **No two people have the exact same experience**, which is why people sometimes feel misunderstood.

**Carl Rogers's 3 factors for understanding between people**

| Factor | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **1. Congruence** | Being **authentic, real, transparent**; your behavior matches your feelings. Breaks the barrier between sender and receiver |
| **2. Unconditional positive regard** | **Accepting a person** regardless of their performance, traits, or past; keeping an open mind |
| **3. Empathic understanding** | **Setting aside your ego** and putting yourself **in their shoes**; never seeing yourself as above or below anyone |

**Examples:** **₱500** is cheap for a well-off person but expensive for someone struggling; **rain** is a happy class suspension for some but a flood for others; school is fun for some, not for others.

> **Summary:** Put yourself in their shoes. People have different standpoints, so be **flexible** in how you communicate.

### Quick Tradition Identifier

| If the situation is about... | Tradition |
|---|---|
| Persuading/changing attitudes, cause and effect, credibility | **Socio-Psychological** |
| Message getting distorted, feedback, noise, telephone game | **Cybernetic** |
| Public speaking, speeches, persuasion to an audience | **Rhetorical** |
| Signs, symbols, emojis, words with many meanings | **Semiotic** |
| Culture shaping meaning (red = stop, rice) | **Socio-Cultural** |
| Power, dominance, oppression, media influence | **Critical** |
| Personal experience, perspective, "in their shoes" | **Phenomenological** |
